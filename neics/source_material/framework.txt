RISK QUANTIFICATION
The Cost of Misclassification — Why Accuracy Matters
Misclassification is not a clerical issue. It propagates through every downstream statistical product, fiscal aggregate, and international report — and is difficult to detect or reverse once embedded.
COMMON MISCLASSIFICATION SYMPTOMS
Government-controlled corporations treated as private
→ Public sector understated
Public corporations recorded inside general government
→ Government expenditure overstated
Non-market producers treated as market producers
→ Output misvalued; subsidies hidden
Foreign-controlled firms classified as domestic
→ FDI statistics distorted
Holding companies treated as operating entities
→ Activity (ISIC) misallocated
Family conglomerates not consolidated as a group
→ Concentration metrics unreliable
QFC/free-zone units treated as non-resident
→ GDP coverage incomplete
DOWNSTREAM CONSEQUENCES
GDP composition
Sectoral shares distort diversification narrative
Fiscal aggregates
GFS reporting mis-states government footprint
Monetary statistics
Financial-corporations sector incorrectly bounded
Balance of payments

===== SLIDE 8 =====
FOUNDATIONAL PRINCIPLES
Eight Guiding Principles of the National Framework
01
Economic Reality over Legal Form
Classification reflects how an entity actually behaves and is controlled, not how it is labelled.
02
International Alignment
Anchored in SNA 2025, IMF GFS 2014, OECD BD4, ISIC Rev.4, LEI, SDMX, GSIM & GSBPM — explicitly and traceably.
03
Ownership Traceability
Every entity traced to its ultimate controlling unit and, where relevant, ultimate beneficial owner.
04
Single Statistical Truth
One classification per entity, governed centrally, used by all statistical and fiscal users.
05
Decision Auditability
Every classification decision is documented, versioned, justified, and reproducible.
06
Source Hierarchy
Conflicts between sources resolved through a defined hierarchy and statistical-override rules.
07
Periodic Re-examination
Classification is not static; entities reviewed on a defined cycle and on triggers.
08
Confidentiality with Transparency
Methodology is public; entity-level decisions are confidential and protected by law.
Cross-cutting principle: A classification is defensible if and only if the underlying evidence trail can be reproduced by any other competent statistician.

1. Identify all activities
2. Measure value-added share
3. Designate principal
4. List secondary

Principal activity
The activity that generates the largest share of value added — NOT the largest share of revenue or employment alone. If value added cannot be measured directly, use gross output as a proxy.

TEST 7 OF 18
Market vs Non-Market Producer Test — The 50 % Rule
A producer is classified as 'market' if its sales cover more than 50 % of its production costs over a sustained period — the 'economically significant prices' test.
THE 50% RULE
Sales
>  50 %
Production Costs
→  MARKET PRODUCER
Public corporation if government-controlled; private otherwise
Otherwise  →  NON-MARKET PRODUCER
General government (S.13) if controlled by government; NPISH (S.15) otherwise
Operational Definitions
Sales
Receipts from third parties at prices that materially influence demand and supply
Production costs
Intermediate consumption + compensation of employees + consumption of fixed capital + other taxes on production
Exclude from sales
Sales to government at prices set to cover costs without competition (not economically significant)
Sustained period
Test must hold for three or more consecutive years; one-off swings are disregarded
Qatar adjustments
Exclude in-kind transfers, intra-government sales, regulated tariffs that do not respond to demand

===== SLIDE 25 =====
TEST 8 OF 18 — PART A
Ownership & Effective Control — Core Concepts
Control is not a synonym for ownership. An entity may be controlled with less than majority ownership — or, conversely, may be majority-owned without being controlled. Statistical classification follows substance, not paper.
OWNERSHIP
Legal title to equity. Necessary input but not sufficient evidence of control.
Measure: % of shares held
CONTROL
The ability to determine the general policy of an entity (financing, investment, dividends, senior appointments).
Measure: voting rights, board, contractual rights, financing dependency
INFLUENCE
Significant power short of control — typically associated holdings of 20-50 % or strategic agreements.
Treatment: separate flag; not consolidated
THE CENTRAL PROPOSITION OF THIS FRAMEWORK
A company may legally appear private but be statistically classified as government-controlled if effective control exists through direct or indirect government influence, board appointment rights, contractual arrangements, or financing dependency.

===== SLIDE 26 =====
TEST 8 OF 18 — PART B
Indicators of Effective Control
Effective control is established when one or more of the following indicators is present. Indicators may operate jointly; weighting depends on context.
1
MAJORITY VOTING RIGHTS
Direct or indirect holding of more than 50% of voting rights in the entity.
2
BOARD APPOINTMENT RIGHTS
Power to appoint, remove, or veto the majority of the board or senior management.
3
GOLDEN SHARES & VETO RIGHTS
Special shares granting veto over strategic decisions regardless of equity stake held.
4
CONTRACTUAL CONTROL
Management contracts, concession agreements, or shareholder agreements conferring decisive control.
5
FINANCING DEPENDENCY
Sole or dominant lender/guarantor with conditions enabling effective control over policy.
6
DOMINANT CUSTOMER OR SUPPLIER
Government as exclusive purchaser or supplier creating structural dependence.
7
REGULATORY CONTROL
Sector-specific rules conferring authority beyond ordinary supervision (price, output, entry).
8
BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP CHAIN
Control exercised through layered or beneficial ownership structures, including SPVs.
A ninth indicator — power to appoint, remove, or set the remuneration of key personnel (CEO/CFO) — is assessed alongside these eight. Decision rule: any single indicator may suffice when sufficiently strong; the totality must be assessed.

===== SLIDE 29 =====
TEST 11 OF 18
Enterprise Group Boundary & Consolidation Rules
Every entity is classified as an institutional unit on a standalone basis; in parallel, it is associated with an enterprise group. Both perspectives are needed for national accounts and policy analysis.
DEFINING THE ENTERPRISE GROUP
An association of legal units bound together by control links.
Global ultimate parent
Topmost entity in the chain — may be resident or non-resident.
Domestic group head
Highest resident entity in Qatar — relevant for national accounts.
Subsidiaries
Entities directly or indirectly controlled by the group head.
Associates
Holdings of 20–50% recorded separately; not consolidated.
Special purpose entities
SPVs and conduits — included only if substantive activity exists.
CONSOLIDATION RULES FOR STATISTICS
Statistical consolidation is NOT identical to financial consolidation.
Each institutional unit reports independently to the national accounts.
Intra-group transactions are preserved (not eliminated) for production accounts.
The enterprise-group view enables analysis of concentration and ownership exposure.
For financial corporations, prudential consolidation (QCB / IFRS) is recorded in parallel.
For public corporations, the group view aligns with the public-sector boundary (GFS 2014).

TEST 12 OF 18
Foreign Ownership & the FDI 10% Threshold
The OECD Benchmark Definition (4th edition) treats a 10% voting-rights threshold as the operational boundary between direct and portfolio investment. Qatar adopts this threshold while assessing control on substance.
< 10 %
Portfolio Investment
Recorded under portfolio flows / balance of payments. No FDI relationship.
10 – 50 %
Direct Investment — Associate
FDI relationship established. Treated as influence; not consolidated.
> 50 %
Direct Investment — Subsidiary
Majority control. Consolidated in group view; classified by control test.
QATAR-SPECIFIC CONSIDERATIONS
QFC entities —
Foreign-incorporated parent but resident in Qatar — FDI flag applied; included in resident enterprise universe.
Free-zone entities —
100% foreign ownership permitted; treated as resident producers; sector and FDI flags both apply.
Joint ventures —

AI & DIGITAL OWNERSHIP
Targeted Automation in the Classification Workflow

METHOD
Graph database + cycle detection + percentage propagation with audit trail.

METHOD
LLM-assisted extraction with structured templates and analyst review of every output.
ANOMALY DETECTION
Flagging entities whose attributes drift from expected patterns over time or relative to peers.
METHOD
Statistical outlier detection on the CSBR snapshot; case review by analyst.
HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP — every AI suggestion is reviewed; no classification is committed by an algorithm alone.
