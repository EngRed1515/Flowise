===== SLIDE 2 =====
WHERE THE STORY BEGINS
One Entity. Many National Numbers.
Before any statistic can be produced, one question must be answered: what exactly is this entity?
Every enterprise in Qatar feeds multiple official statistics at once. If it is placed in the wrong box, every number that depends on it inherits the error.
GDP & sector accounts
Output and value added land in the wrong industry or sector
Public finances
An entity wrongly inside — or outside — government distorts the fiscal picture
Foreign investment
Misreading control misstates inward and outward FDI
Debt & credibility
Public-sector debt and ratings rest on the boundary being right
The cost of getting it wrong is not one number — it is a chain of them.  That is why classification is treated as a discipline, not a label.

===== SLIDE 3 =====
THE ONE RULE
We Classify by Reality, Not by Label
So we never take an entity at its word. We look at what it is, not what it is called.
An entity is classified by how it behaves and who truly controls it — not by its registration label, its name, or its own description.
What we ignore
Commercial-registration label · legal name · self-description · public perception
What we examine
Ownership chains · effective control · market behaviour · governance · beneficial owners
A company can look private on paper and be government-controlled in substance — and the reverse. Substance decides.

Who effectively controls it — and is that the State?

===== SLIDE 12 =====
THE DECISIVE QUESTION
Who Really Controls It? Nine Tests for Control
Control rarely shows on the share register alone. We look nine ways — any one can be decisive.
1
Majority voting rights
2
Power to appoint or remove the board
3
Golden share / veto rights
4
Decisive contractual control
5
Financing dependency
6
Dominant customer or supplier
7
Sector-specific regulatory control
8
Beneficial-ownership chain
9
Power over key personnel (CEO/CFO)
This is why a “private” company can be public.  A minority stake — even no equity — combined with board appointment or a golden share can place an entity firmly inside the public sector.
